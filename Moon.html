The Moon

Facts about the Moon

-The Moon is Earth's only Moon

-It is about 238,855 miles away from Earth

-It is about 2,159 miles wide, roughly a quarter of the size of Earth

-It takes about 27 days to orbit Earth

-The same side of the Moon always faces Earth

-Twelve people have walked on the Moon

The Red Moon

Sometimes the Moon turns a deep red, which is why a total lunar eclipse is often called a "blood moon." It happens when Earth moves directly between the Sun and the Moon, so Earth's shadow falls across the Moon.

The Moon does not go completely dark, because some sunlight still bends around the edges of Earth through our atmosphere. The air scatters away the blue light, and the red and orange light gets through and shines onto the Moon. It is the same reason sunsets look red.

The exact shade can change from eclipse to eclipse, depending on how much dust and cloud is in Earth's atmosphere.
